Getting married in Australia

Legal requirements & paperwork

The legal side is simpler than it sounds — and I'll handle the heavy lifting. Here's the step-by-step:

  1. Check you're eligible. You both need to be at least 18, not already married, not closely related, and genuinely consent to the marriage.
  2. Lodge your Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM). This must be given to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before your wedding date.
  3. Show your documents. Evidence of your date and place of birth (a birth certificate or passport) and photo ID. If either of you has been married before, you'll also need a divorce certificate or death certificate.
  4. Sign the Declaration of No Legal Impediment. We'll do this together before the ceremony — often at the rehearsal or just before you walk down the aisle.
  5. Bring two witnesses. Anyone aged 18 or over who can understand the ceremony.
  6. Say the legal words. I'll include the required wording and your legal vows — everything else is completely up to you.
  7. Sign the marriage certificates. You, your witnesses and I sign three certificates on the day.
  8. Registration. I lodge your marriage with Queensland Births, Deaths & Marriages within 14 days.
  9. Order your official certificate. The certificate you receive on the day is ceremonial — apply to BDM for the official one (you'll need it to change your name).

Can't wait a month? In limited circumstances a prescribed authority can shorten the notice period — ask me about it.

This is general information only. Always check the Attorney-General's Department for the latest requirements.

Costs & timelines

A rough timeline

  1. 12–18 months out. Lock in your date, venue and celebrant — peak spring and autumn Saturdays go quickly.
  2. Up to 18 months (and at least 1 month) out. Lodge your Notice of Intended Marriage.
  3. 3–6 months out. Planning meeting, love-story questionnaire and ceremony ideas.
  4. 1–2 months out. Finalise your ceremony, readings, vows and music cues.
  5. The week of. Rehearsal and sign your Declaration of No Legal Impediment.
  6. Within 14 days after. I register your marriage — then you can order your official certificate.

Same-sex marriage & equality

Marriage equality has been law in Australia since December 2017, and every couple follows exactly the same legal process — same paperwork, same timelines, same joy.

Good questions

Frequently asked questions

Do we need to be Australian citizens or residents?

No — you don't need to be a citizen or resident to marry in Australia. You just need to meet the legal requirements and give at least one month's notice.

Can we get married anywhere?

Yes! Beach, park, backyard, venue or mountaintop — anywhere in Australia, as long as your celebrant and two witnesses are there.

Can we write our own vows?

Absolutely. There's a short set of legal words we need to include — everything else can be as personal as you like. I'm happy to help you write them.

Do you travel outside the Gold Coast?

I love an adventure. I'm based on the Gold Coast and happily travel to surrounding areas and beyond — a travel fee may apply.

Is the certificate we sign on the day the official one?

Not quite — it's a beautiful keepsake. Once I register your marriage you can order the official certificate from Births, Deaths & Marriages.

How far ahead should we book?

12–18 months is ideal for popular dates, but get in touch whatever your timeline — I'll always do my best to make it work.
